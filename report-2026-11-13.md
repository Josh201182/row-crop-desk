# Row Crop Desk — November 13, 2026

Overnight trade on Friday, November 13, shows grains firming after Thursday’s lower closes. December corn is near $4.46–$4.48, January soybeans near $12.33–$12.37, and Chicago wheat near $5.91–$5.95. ICE January canola settled Thursday at C$777.30 and is trading in the mid-to-upper C$780s overnight. The November 10 WASDE trimmed corn and wheat season-average farm prices while lifting soybeans; the board now sits modestly under the new corn and wheat anchors and holds a premium in soybeans.

## Direction

Nearby futures bounced overnight after two lower sessions in corn and a broader Thursday decline. The November WASDE (WASDE-677, released November 10) cut the U.S. corn yield to 179.6 bu/acre, production to 15.893 billion bushels, and ending stocks to 1.758 billion, while lowering the season-average farm price 10 cents to $4.60. Soybean yield rose to 53.3, production to 4.574 billion, ending stocks fell to 300 million on stronger exports, and the farm price rose 10 cents to $12.10. Wheat ending stocks edged up to 743 million and the farm price fell 10 cents to $6.20. Canola continues to track the soy complex and soyoil, recovering from a four-month low near C$743 earlier in the week.

## Horizons

- Short, 2–6 weeks: firmer near term after the WASDE, but still sensitive to harvest completion and basis. December corn is recovering toward the mid-$4.40s to low-$4.50s. January canola is holding above the mid-C$770s after testing lower. A sustained soyoil or crude rally, or fresh export sales, is the main upside; residual harvest flow and fund liquidation remain downside risks.
- Mid, into Q1 2027: mixed and conditional. The tighter soybean balance sheet supports the premium; corn remains more amply supplied. South American weather (especially Brazil), Chinese demand pace, and crush margins are the swing factors. Canadian canola crush capacity continues to absorb a larger share of the crop; Agriculture Canada’s October update held ending stocks near 1.97 million tonnes and raised the average price forecast to $800/tonne.
- Long, season average: November USDA anchors of $4.60 corn, $12.10 soybeans, and $6.20 wheat remain above the last decade’s realized averages and above the 2025/26 final MYAs. Nearby futures sit under the corn and wheat anchors and over the soybean anchor. World Bank outlooks call for further gains in 2027.

## Marketing read

Do not chase the overnight firming in corn. Scale sales only where basis improves and December holds the mid-$4.40s; keep a portion for a later demand or weather story. Soybeans near $12.35 carry a solid premium to the $12.10 farm-price forecast—do not empty the book on residual harvest pressure alone. Sell wheat rallies until export shipments confirm demand. Canola can be held or lightly sold while the mid-to-upper C$770s hold; a sustained break under that level would change the near-term tone.
