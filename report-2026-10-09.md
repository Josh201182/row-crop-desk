Those gaps are not pure basis. The farm price is a marketing-year average, cash, after basis. A December future above $4.80 does not mean USDA is already too low. It does mean the board is not trading the heavy-supply case that was in the spring baselines.

September balance sheet, still the working official set until noon:

- Corn: production 15.8 billion bushels, down 213 million from August; yield 178.5 bu/acre; exports 3.3 billion; ending stocks 1.6 billion; farm price up 30 cents to $4.80.
- Soybeans: production about 4.5 billion bushels; yield 52.8; exports 1.69 billion; ending stocks 310 million; farm price up 60 cents to $12.00. Meal $340 a short ton. Oil 70 cents a pound.
- Wheat: U.S. supply and use unchanged; farm price up 20 cents to $6.40. Global wheat supplies were raised. World corn stocks were not.

Reuters-surveyed analysts going into today looked for corn yield near 177.7 bu/acre and production near 15.72 billion, a token cut from September. Soybean yield was expected unchanged at 52.8, production near 4.53 billion, above last year’s 4.26 billion. A small corn cut is mostly in the price. A real cut, or a stocks surprise, is the friendly risk. A yield hold with harvest running is the soft risk.

Export sales for the week ending October 1, as carried by Farm Futures: corn 31.2 million bushels, up 44% on the week; soybeans 20.2 million, 34% under the four-week average; wheat 16.6 million, up 56% on the week. Shipments were 62.9 million corn, 50.7 million soybeans, and only 8 million wheat. China was on the soybean destination list, but the August summit did not settle the book, and that is why November gave up 10 cents.

## Horizons

- Short, 2–6 weeks: soft to sideways in the grains, firmer in canola only while the veg-oil and crude bid holds. Harvest weather is open across much of the Corn Belt into the weekend. December corn support cited earlier in the week near $4.88 is still the line. November canola support is still the C$800 area; Thursday’s low did not test it. Mark Jankowski’s seasonal-rise comment in some wires is from the April WASDE cycle and is not today’s outlook.
- Mid, into Q1 2027: constructive for corn and soybeans if Brazilian weather slips and U.S. crush stays at a record. Successful Farming on October 1 still has the timing analogue of a June 2026 low and a possible high as early as December 2026–March 2027. That is a calendar rhyme with 2012, not a price rhyme. CoBank’s Tanner Ehmke, writing October 7, had corn up about 20% in the prior quarter on a smaller U.S. crop. That quarterly gain is intact after two down sessions.
- Long, the season average: September anchors of $4.80 corn, $12.00 soybeans, and $6.40 wheat sit above both the March FAPRI 2026/27 baseline ($4.21 / $10.39 / $5.58) and the last decade of realized season averages. Canola is the oilseed that AAFC has been marking up, not down. Kevin Hursh, September 28, noted AAFC lifted this crop year’s average canola price by $55 a tonne on the month and put it more than $60 above last year. Futures at C$816 are consistent with that upgrade, not with a surplus story.

FAPRI’s 2015/16–2024/25 averages were $4.34 corn, $10.55 soybeans, and $5.72 wheat. The 2014–2019 trough lived mostly at $3.50–$4.00 corn and $8–$10 soybeans. The 2021–22 spike is the modern ceiling in the farm-price series. Nearby futures are in the upper half of the post-2020 range: above the 2026 spring insurance prices of $4.62 corn and $11.19 soybeans, and well under the 2012 futures peaks near $8.40 corn and $17.90 soybeans.

Against that decade, the September 2026/27 farm-price forecasts are rich: corn $4.80 is about 46 cents over the ten-year average, soybeans $12.00 about $1.45 over, wheat $6.40 about 68 cents over. The board is richer still. The 2025/26 projected MYAs ($4.15 corn, $10.40 soybeans, $5.05 wheat) show how far the new-crop story has already moved off last year’s realized path.

Canola: November at C$815.90 is about 3% under the recent C$843 area and about 8% above the early-August low near C$755. Crush margins were quoted Thursday morning near C$296–$299 a tonne over the November. That margin is the floor under the flat price.

## Marketing read

Do not chase the two-day grain dip, and do not assume noon rewrites it. Scale corn if December holds $5.00 and basis is workable; keep a piece for a yield cut or a Brazilian scare. Soybeans at $12.88 on a 310-million-bushel stocks figure are not a record-crop liquidation. Sell wheat rallies until shipments, not just sales, catch the U.S. price. Canola can be held while C$800 holds and the crush margin stays near C$300.
