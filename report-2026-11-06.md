# Row Crop Desk — November 6, 2026

Harvest pressure remains the dominant force as the U.S. corn and soybean harvest wraps up. Overnight trade on Friday, November 6, shows December corn near $4.40, January soybeans near $11.90, and Chicago wheat near $5.88. ICE January canola settled Thursday at C$753.20 and is testing lower overnight after breaking its 200-day moving average. The board now sits clearly under the October USDA season-average farm prices for corn, soybeans, and wheat.

Nearby futures continue soft under advancing harvest, larger U.S. supplies from the October 9 WASDE, and limited fresh demand news. Wheat is softer overnight on peace-talk headlines and non-threatening southern Plains weather. Canola has seen heavier speculative long liquidation.

## Horizons

- Short, 2–6 weeks: soft to sideways while remaining harvest finishes and basis stays wide. December corn is testing the lower end of the post-WASDE range near $4.40. January canola is defending near C$750 after the break. A sustained crude or vegetable-oil rally, or a fresh large export sale, is the main upside risk; open weather and continued fund liquidation are the downside. November 10 WASDE is the next scheduled catalyst.
- Mid, into Q1 2027: mixed and conditional. Larger U.S. supplies cap rallies, but South American weather (especially Brazil), Chinese demand pace, and crush margins remain the swing factors. Soybeans still show tighter stocks-to-use than corn. Canadian canola crush capacity continues to absorb a larger share of the crop; Agriculture Canada’s October update raised the average price forecast to $800/tonne on ending stocks near 1.97 million tonnes. World Bank projections point to higher grain and oilseed prices into 2027 on supply risks and biofuel demand.
- Long, season average: October USDA anchors of $4.70 corn, $12.00 soybeans, and $6.30 wheat remain above the last decade’s realized averages and above the 2025/26 final MYAs. Nearby futures have now moved under those anchors across the three grains.

## Marketing read

Do not chase the further decline in corn. Scale sales only where basis improves and December holds the mid-$4.40s; keep a portion for a later demand or weather story. Soybeans near $11.90 have moved under the $12.00 farm-price forecast—do not empty the book on harvest pressure alone, but the premium is gone. Sell wheat rallies until export shipments confirm demand. Canola can be held or lightly sold while support near C$750 holds; a sustained break under that level would change the near-term tone.
