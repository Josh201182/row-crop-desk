# Row Crop Desk — Market Briefing
**As of October 1, 2026** · Corn, soybeans, wheat, canola
*Commentary for merchandisers and producers. Not a solicitation to buy or sell.*

## Tape
The intermediate trend from early August into mid-September was **up**. CME Group reported continuous corn and oats each up more than 12% from early August through mid-September, with Kansas City wheat, soybeans, and soybean meal each up more than 8%, on geopolitics, weather, and tighter-than-feared supply forecasts.

The short-term trend into October 1 is **down**. Harvest pressure and a September 30 quarterly-stocks reaction pulled futures off the mid-September highs.

National basis, Sep 29 snapshot: corn about 30 under December; soybeans about 29 over November. Cash corn in the Sep 22 window was estimated near $4.92, roughly 45 cents under December at the time.

## What changed in the September 11 WASDE
- **Corn:** Yield cut 2.2 bu to 178.5 bu/acre. Production 15.8 billion bushels, down 213 million. Ending stocks about 1.567 billion bushels. Season-average price raised to **$4.80**. Support came from a smaller crop, not from stronger use. Exports left at 3.3 billion bushels.
- **Soybeans:** Production edged higher, but demand absorbed it. Ending stocks 310 million bushels; stocks-to-use about 6.8%. Season-average price raised to **$12.00**. Meal raised to $340/short ton. Oil left at 70 cents/lb. Support came from demand.
- **Wheat:** Aggregate U.S. supply and use unchanged. Ending stocks 717 million bushels; stocks-to-use about 38.3%. Season-average price raised to **$6.40** on reported prices, not on a tighter sheet. Global wheat supplies were raised.

Harvest progress on Sep 21: corn 13% (5-year 11%), soybeans 12% (5-year 8%), spring wheat essentially done.

## Versus history
Season-average farm prices, marketing year:

- **Corn:** 2021/22 about $6.00; 2022/23 $6.54; 2023/24 $4.55; 2024/25 $4.24; 2025/26 near $4.00 on earlier sheets; **2026/27 projection $4.80**. Above the last three years, far below the 2021–23 spike. Stocks near 1.57 billion are tighter than the early-2026 fear of a 2-billion carryout and tighter than the mid-2010s gluts, but not 2012-tight.
- **Soybeans:** 2021/22 about $13.30; 2022/23 $14.20; 2023/24 $12.40; 2024/25 $10.00; 2025/26 about $10.50; **2026/27 projection $12.00**. A real recovery, still short of the spike. A 6.8% stocks-to-use ratio is back in the historically snug band.
- **Wheat:** 2021/22 about $7.63; 2022/23 $8.83; **2026/27 projection $6.40**. Price recovered from an early-2026 idea near $5.00, but a ~38% stocks-to-use ratio says the world sheet is still comfortable.
- **Canola:** November futures near C$810–825 in late September are well above Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada’s earlier 2026/27 season-average idea of about C$640/t (versus about C$665 the prior year). AAFC late September raised 2026/27 carryout to about 1.98 million tonnes from 1.50 million in August. China bought Australian canola for the first time since 2020.

## Projections
**Short (2–6 weeks).** Seasonal and defensive. Harvest is accelerating, corn basis is wide, and the board has already given back a large piece of the August–mid-September rally. Rallies are vulnerable until combines slow and weekly export sales confirm. China soybean commitments — the White House has cited at least 25 million metric tons of U.S. soybeans during 2026 — are the week-to-week swing.

**Mid (2026/27 marketing year).** USDA is the working anchor: corn $4.80, soybeans $12.00, wheat $6.40. Those are not ceilings. September already revised all three higher, and another corn yield cut or a real Chinese shipping program can retake the mid-September highs. They are not floors if South America plants and finishes a record soybean crop. Canola’s mid-year price tracks soyoil, crude, and whether China keeps splitting purchases toward Australia.

**Long (beyond this marketing year).** Three structures dominate. Brazil continues to take U.S. soybean export share even when U.S. exports recover. Global wheat stays well supplied unless a Black Sea or other major-exporter shock hits. Domestic crush, renewable diesel, and food-oil demand are the slow bid under soybeans and canola.

## Desk actions this week
1. Do not chase the August rally with new length into harvest.
2. Trade basis, not just flat price. Corn is the wide-basis crop; nearby soybeans have been the firm-basis crop.
3. Scale sales against the USDA season averages and keep a weather and China option open into the **October 9 WASDE**.
