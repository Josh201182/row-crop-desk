## Balance sheet (September 11 WASDE)

- Corn: production cut 213 million bushels to 15.8 billion; yield 178.5 bu/acre; ending stocks 1.6 billion; season-average farm price $4.80, up 30 cents.
- Soybeans: production 4.5 billion bushels; exports 1.69 billion; ending stocks 310 million; farm price $12.00, up 60 cents.
- Wheat: U.S. supply and use unchanged; farm price $6.40, up 20 cents. Global wheat stocks increased. World corn stocks declined.

Trade heading into October 9 looks for corn yield near 177.7 bu/acre. A small cut is mostly priced. A larger cut, with record August corn exports and firm ethanol, would be the friendly surprise.

## Horizons

- Short (2–6 weeks): soft to sideways. Harvest pressure, pre-report de-risking, canola support at C$800. December corn support cited near $4.88.
- Mid (into Q1 2027): constructive for corn and soybeans if El Niño disrupts Brazilian planting and crush stays record. Successful Farming (Oct 1) flagged a June 2026 low and a possible high as early as December 2026–March 2027. Timing analogue is 2012, not the price.
- Long: September farm-price anchors ($4.80 corn, $12.00 soybeans, $6.40 wheat) are already far above the March FAPRI 2026/27 baseline ($4.21 / $10.39 / $5.58). FAPRI's 2015/16–2024/25 averages were $4.34 corn, $10.55 soybeans, $5.72 wheat. The board is above the last decade, not a model-guaranteed plateau.

## Versus history

Spring 2026 crop-insurance prices were $4.62 corn and $11.19 soybeans. Nearby futures are well above those guarantees. The 2014–2019 trough lived mostly at $3.50–$4.00 corn and $8–$10 soybeans. 2021–22 was the modern spike. 2012 futures peaked near $8.40 corn and $17.90 soybeans. This market is in the upper half of the post-2020 range.

Canola: about 3% under the recent C$843 high and 8% above the early-August low near C$755. AAFC has been raising, not cutting, the crop-year price outlook. Crush margins near C$300/t are the floor under the story.

## Marketing read

Scale corn into strength above $5.00 December if basis is acceptable; keep a piece for a yield cut or a Brazilian scare. Do not dump the soybean book on a record crop — 310 million bushels and record crush are why November is near $13, not $10. Sell wheat rallies until exports prove the U.S. price. Canola holds while C$800 holds.
