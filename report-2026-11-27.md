# Row Crop Desk — November 27, 2026

Nearby grain futures settled firmer on Wednesday, November 25, before the Thanksgiving holiday pause. December 2026 corn closed at $4.39¾, January 2027 soybeans at $12.44, and December 2026 Chicago wheat at $5.65½. ICE January canola has been trading in the mid-to-upper C$760s to C$770s, with recent prints near C$766–$770 after thin holiday trade. The November 10 WASDE left 2026/27 season-average farm prices at $4.60 corn, $12.10 soybeans, and $6.20 wheat; the board has recovered some of the post-WASDE softness, putting corn and soybeans nearer the anchors while wheat remains discounted.

## Direction

Wednesday’s session saw corn and soybeans advance on short covering and firm meal, while wheat eased. December corn gained 5¾¢ to $4.39¾; January soybeans rose 10¢ to $12.44; December wheat fell 4¼¢ to $5.65½. Deferred contracts followed, with December 2027 corn near $4.84½ and November 2027 soybeans near $11.85¾. Markets were closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and expected lighter volume Friday. Canola saw thin trade and modest weakness mid-week, remaining rangebound below Agriculture Canada’s $800 outlook.

## Horizons

- Short, 2–6 weeks: mixed to firmer. December corn is holding the upper $4.30s after the Wednesday bounce. January soybeans have reclaimed the mid-$12.40s and sit above the farm-price forecast. Wheat remains capped under $5.70–$5.80. Canola is holding the mid-to-upper C$760s. Export sales (delayed to Friday), South American weather, and residual holiday positioning are the near-term drivers. A firmer dollar or renewed fund liquidation remains a downside risk.
- Mid, into Q1 2027: mixed and conditional. The tighter soybean balance sheet continues to support values above the farm-price forecast; corn remains more amply supplied and under its anchor. Brazilian weather, Chinese purchase pace, and crush margins are the key variables. Canadian canola crush capacity continues to absorb a larger share of the crop. Farmer selling after year-end tax considerations could weigh on canola into January–February.
- Long, season average: November USDA anchors of $4.60 corn, $12.10 soybeans, and $6.20 wheat remain above the last decade’s realized averages and above the 2025/26 final MYAs. Nearby futures sit under the corn and wheat anchors and above the soybean anchor. World Bank outlooks call for further gains in 2027; USDA long-term baselines see prices remaining relatively stable but below the 2021–22 highs.

## Marketing read

Corn has recovered into the upper $4.30s but remains under the $4.60 season-average anchor; scale sales only where basis improves and the mid-$4.30s hold. Soybeans near $12.44 carry a premium to the $12.10 farm-price forecast—do not empty the book on this bounce alone, but residual harvest pressure has eased. Sell wheat rallies until export shipments confirm demand; the board sits farther under its anchor. Canola can be held or lightly sold while the mid-to-upper C$760s hold; a sustained break under that level would change the near-term tone.
