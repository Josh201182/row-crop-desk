## Horizons

- Short, 2–6 weeks: soft to sideways while harvest finishes and basis stays wide. December corn is testing the lower end of the post-WASDE range near $4.58–4.60. November canola is defending the mid-C$770s. A sustained crude or vegetable-oil rally, or a fresh large export sale, is the main upside risk; open weather and continued fund liquidation are the downside.
- Mid, into Q1 2027: mixed and conditional. Larger U.S. supplies cap rallies, but South American weather (especially Brazil), Chinese demand pace, and crush margins remain the swing factors. Soybeans still show tighter stocks-to-use than corn. Canadian canola crush capacity continues to absorb a larger share of the crop; Agriculture Canada has raised its price outlook toward C$800/tonne on tighter ending stocks.
- Long, season average: October USDA anchors of $4.70 corn, $12.00 soybeans, and $6.30 wheat remain above the last decade’s realized averages and above the 2025/26 final MYAs. Nearby futures have now converged closer to (or slightly under) those anchors for corn and wheat.

## Marketing read

Do not chase the further decline in corn. Scale sales only where basis improves and December holds the mid-$4.50s; keep a portion for a later demand or weather story. Soybeans near $12.53 still carry a premium to the $12.00 farm-price forecast—do not empty the book on harvest pressure alone. Sell wheat rallies until export shipments confirm demand. Canola can be held or lightly sold while the mid-C$770s hold; a sustained break under that level would change the near-term tone.
