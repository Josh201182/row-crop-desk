## Horizons

- Short, 2–6 weeks: mixed and rangebound. December corn is testing the mid-$4.30s after failing to hold the mid-$4.40s. January soybeans are consolidating near $12.10–$12.15. Wheat remains capped under $5.90. Canola is holding the mid-to-upper C$770s. A sustained soyoil or crude rally, or fresh export sales, is the main upside; residual harvest flow, a firmer dollar, and fund liquidation remain downside risks. South American weather is the near-term swing factor.
- Mid, into Q1 2027: mixed and conditional. The tighter soybean balance sheet still supports values near the farm-price forecast; corn remains more amply supplied. Brazilian weather, Chinese demand pace, and crush margins are the key variables. Canadian canola crush capacity continues to absorb a larger share of the crop; Agriculture Canada’s October update held ending stocks near 1.97 million tonnes and raised the average price forecast to $800/tonne. Farmer selling after year-end tax considerations could weigh on canola into January–February.
- Long, season average: November USDA anchors of $4.60 corn, $12.10 soybeans, and $6.20 wheat remain above the last decade’s realized averages and above the 2025/26 final MYAs. Nearby futures sit under the corn and wheat anchors and roughly in line with the soybean anchor. World Bank outlooks call for further gains in 2027; USDA long-term baselines see prices remaining relatively stable but below the 2021–22 highs.

## Marketing read

Do not chase any residual firmness in corn. Scale sales only where basis improves and December holds the mid-$4.30s; keep a portion for a later demand or weather story. Soybeans near $12.12 are roughly in line with the $12.10 farm-price forecast—do not empty the book on residual harvest pressure alone, but the earlier premium has faded. Sell wheat rallies until export shipments confirm demand. Canola can be held or lightly sold while the mid-to-upper C$770s hold; a sustained break under that level would change the near-term tone.
