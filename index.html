Concise report

The intermediate trend from early August into mid-September was up. CME Group reported continuous corn up more than 12% and soybeans up more than 8% over that stretch. The short-term trend into October 1 is down: December corn near $5.01, November soybeans near $12.91, December soft red wheat near $6.78, and November canola near C$811 after a C$824.30 close on September 22.

September 11 WASDE (WASDE-675) raised season-average farm prices because ending stocks tightened. Corn yield was cut to 178.5 bu/acre and production to 15.8 billion bushels; the season average rose 30 cents to $4.80. Soybean ending stocks fell to 310 million bushels (stocks-to-use about 6.8%) and the season average rose 60 cents to $12.00, on demand rather than a smaller crop. Wheat supply and use were unchanged, stocks-to-use about 38%, and the season average rose 20 cents to $6.40.

Horizons

  • Short, 2–6 weeks. Defensive. Harvest is accelerating, corn basis is wide (about 30 under December on a Sep 29 national snapshot; soybeans were about 29 over November). Rallies are vulnerable until export sales confirm, especially the cited 25 million metric ton China soybean commitment for 2026.
  • Mid, 2026/27. Working anchors are $4.80 corn, $12 soybeans, and $6.40 wheat. Not ceilings if yields slip further or China ships. Not floors if South America finishes a record soybean crop. Canola tracks soyoil, crude, and Australian competition into China.
  • Long. Brazil keeps taking U.S. soybean export share. Global wheat stays comfortable barring a major exporter shock. Crush and renewable-diesel demand are the slow bid under soybeans and canola.
