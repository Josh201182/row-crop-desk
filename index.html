Concise report · October 8

Direction depends on the window. Nearby futures are down on harvest pressure and pre-report de-risking. The quarter is up: CoBank’s Tanner Ehmke, writing October 7, said corn rose about 20% last quarter as yield prospects sank. September WASDE put the U.S. corn crop at 15.8 billion bushels, yield at 178.5, ending stocks at 1.6 billion, and the season-average farm price at $4.80, up 30 cents.

Soybeans are a record or near-record crop, about 4.5 billion bushels, with ending stocks pulled to 310 million and the farm price up 60 cents to $12.00. August soybean exports were weak. Crush is the bid. Wheat supply and use were unchanged and the farm price rose 20 cents to $6.40, but U.S. prices are among the world’s highest and August exports were still 23% under a year ago. Canola is rangebound and historically elevated, with crush margins near C$300 a tonne.

Versus history, nearby futures are well above the 2026 spring insurance prices of $4.62 corn and $11.19 soybeans, and well below the 2012 futures peaks near $8.40 corn and $17.90 soybeans. This is the upper half of the post-2020 range.

Marketing read: scale corn above $5 December if basis works, and keep a piece for a yield cut or a Brazilian scare. Do not empty the soybean book on a record crop. Sell wheat rallies until exports prove the U.S. price. Canola holds while C$800 holds.
