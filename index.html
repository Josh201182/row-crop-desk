Concise report · November 13

Nearby grain futures firmed overnight after Thursday’s lower closes. December corn is near $4.46–$4.48, roughly 12–14 cents under the November USDA season-average farm price of $4.60. January soybeans near $12.33–$12.37 carry about a 23–27 cent premium to the $12.10 forecast. Chicago wheat near $5.91–$5.95 sits about 25–29 cents under the $6.20 anchor. January canola is trading in the mid-to-upper C$780s after Thursday’s C$777.30 settle, still under Agriculture Canada’s $800 outlook.

The 2025/26 final marketing-year averages were $4.16 corn and $10.50 soybeans. Against the last decade, the current USDA 2026/27 forecasts still sit above realized averages from the mid-2010s through the early 2020s, though well below the 2021–22 spike. The board has adjusted to the smaller corn crop but remains under the official season averages for corn and wheat while soybeans hold a premium.

Marketing read: do not chase the overnight firming in corn. Scale only where basis improves and the mid-$4.40s hold. Keep soybean inventory; the premium to the farm-price forecast is back. Sell wheat rallies until shipments confirm. Canola holds while support in the mid-to-upper C$770s holds.
