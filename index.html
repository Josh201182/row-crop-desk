Concise report · November 27

Nearby grain futures settled firmer on Wednesday, November 25. December corn closed at $4.39¾, roughly 20 cents under the November USDA season-average farm price of $4.60. January soybeans at $12.44 sit about 34 cents above the $12.10 forecast. Chicago wheat at $5.65½ sits about 54 cents under the $6.20 anchor. January canola has been trading in the mid-to-upper C$760s after thin holiday trade, still under Agriculture Canada’s $800 outlook.

The 2025/26 final marketing-year averages were $4.16 corn and $10.50 soybeans. Against the last decade, the current USDA 2026/27 forecasts still sit above realized averages from the mid-2010s through the early 2020s, though well below the 2021–22 spike. The board has recovered some of the post-WASDE softness; corn remains under its anchor, soybeans have moved to a premium, and wheat sits farther under.

Marketing read: corn recovered but remains under the farm-price anchor—scale only where basis improves. Soybeans carry a premium; do not empty the book on this bounce alone. Sell wheat rallies until shipments confirm. Canola holds while support in the mid-to-upper C$760s holds.
