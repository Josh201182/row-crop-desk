Concise report · October 23

Nearby grain futures remain under harvest pressure. December corn is near $4.59, roughly a dime under the October USDA season-average farm price of $4.70. November soybeans near $12.53 still carry about a 53-cent premium to the unchanged $12.00 forecast. Chicago wheat near $6.25 sits only a nickel under the $6.30 anchor. November canola settled at C$777.50 on Thursday after a modest rebound.

The 2025/26 final marketing-year averages were $4.16 corn and $10.50 soybeans. Against the last decade, the current USDA 2026/27 forecasts still sit above realized averages from the mid-2010s through the early 2020s, though well below the 2021–22 spike. The board has adjusted to the larger corn supply and is now close to the official season averages for corn and wheat.

Marketing read: do not chase the further corn decline. Scale only where basis improves and the mid-$4.50s hold. Keep soybean inventory; the board still carries a premium to the farm-price forecast. Sell wheat rallies until shipments confirm. Canola holds while the mid-C$770s hold.
