Key October balance-sheet changes (2026/27):

- Corn: yield raised 2.7 bu/acre to 181.2; production near 16.0 billion bushels; ending stocks raised to 1.849 billion; farm price cut 10¢ to $4.70. 2025/26 final MYA was $4.16.
- Soybeans: yield +0.3 to 53.1; production 4.562 billion; ending stocks 315 million (+5); farm price held at $12.00. 2025/26 final $10.50.
- Wheat: ending stocks 740 million (+23); farm price cut 10¢ to $6.30.

Harvest is advancing. Fund selling and the larger corn carryout are the near-term weights. China has been a buyer of U.S. soybeans in recent weeks, but volumes have not offset the supply news.

## Horizons

- Short, 2–6 weeks: soft to sideways in the grains while harvest runs and basis stays wide. December corn is testing the lower end of the post-WASDE range. November canola is defending C$800. A weather interruption or fresh export sale is the main upside risk; continued dry, open weather is the downside.
- Mid, into Q1 2027: mixed. Larger U.S. supplies cap rallies, but Brazilian weather, Chinese demand pace, and crush margins remain the swing factors. Soybeans still hold a tighter stocks-to-use than corn. Canola crush capacity continues to absorb a larger share of the Canadian crop.
- Long, season average: October anchors of $4.70 corn, $12.00 soybeans, and $6.30 wheat remain above the last decade’s realized averages and above the 2025/26 final MYAs. They sit closer to the board than the pre-report numbers did.

Nearby futures are inside the post-2020 range and closer to the official season averages than they were a week ago.

Canola: November near C$802 is roughly 5% under the early-September highs near C$840 and testing the C$800 area that had held as support. Agriculture Canada has been marking production and crush higher while keeping prices in the C$700–775 range depending on the month and location basis.

## Marketing read

Do not chase the post-WASDE decline in corn. Scale sales only where basis improves and December holds the mid-$4.70s; keep a portion for a later demand or weather story. Soybeans at $12.60 still carry a premium to the $12.00 farm-price forecast—do not empty the book on harvest pressure alone. Sell wheat rallies until export shipments confirm demand. Canola can be held or lightly sold while C$800 holds; a sustained break under that level would change the near-term tone.
